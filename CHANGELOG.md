# Changelog

All notable changes to CoreFactory. The format follows Keep a Changelog; versions follow semantic versioning.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Coal drills: an ore drill at any level mines coal deposits and gives vanilla charcoal, one per unit taken from the deposit
  (`coal_ore` in `ores.yml`, `DrillRules.mined`). Charcoal is also a component in `items.yml`, so a placed coal ore block drops it too.
- Drill animation: a running drill's model moves up and down into the ground, four keyframes per stroke, and rests when it stops.
- Two-block devices, door-style: the wind, charcoal, petroleum, diesel and nuclear generators, and the ore and petroleum drills
  of level IV and V, stand two blocks tall. The anchor is the lower block, and breaking either half breaks the device. The list
  is `Footprint.TALL_IDS`. Solar panels and the other drills stay one block.
- Sideways picking: a picker placed beside a straight belt takes items from that belt, within its speed. Items it does not take
  keep moving. A belt that turns a corner gives nothing sideways.
- Distinct sounds per device kind: 40 sound files in the resource pack (see the sound set under Changed) (`sounds.json`, generated by
  `tools/sounds/make_sounds.py`), one for each generator kind, drill kind and process machine, plus the turret, press and pylon
  sounds. They are quiet by design. `pylon_hum`, `accumulator_charge`, `belt_hum` and `picker_clunk` are packed but not played yet,
  and `generator_hum` and `drill_run` are no longer played.
- Induction furnace: brass ingots from two copper ingots and one zinc ingot. Its recipes are rows in `processes.yml`, so a new
  recipe for a machine needs no code.
- Potassium nitrate: saltpeter deposits (drilled, then crushed to crude saltpeter) are recrystallised twice in the purifier, to
  potassium nitrate and then to high-purity potassium nitrate.
- Sulfur and zinc: sulfur deposits (crushed to sulfur) and zinc deposits (crushed to zinc ore dust, then roasted to zinc ingots).
- Powder mill: one charcoal, one sulfur and one high-purity potassium nitrate make three high-grade gunpowder.
- Ammunition: the cartridge press makes empty shells from brass. The bullet swaging machine makes regular, armour-piercing,
  incendiary and depleted-uranium bullets. The ammunition assembly makes the matching rounds from a shell, a bullet and gunpowder.
- Precision parts: the metal lathe makes a precision barrel; the component assembly makes the steel frame, the turret mechanism,
  the ammunition feed and the targeting unit.
- Heavy Weapons Assembly: the machine that makes the machine gun turret from its six parts and 8 brass ingots, at 250 kW.
- Machine gun turret: a placeable defence that fires at hostile monsters in range, rotates toward them within its arc (the whole turn by default),
  and uses ammunition and power. Its settings are the `turrets` section of `config.yml`. Right-click it with a round to load it;
  right-click it with no round, or sneak-click it, to open its menu. Belts and pickers feed it rounds. Players, villagers, golems,
  animals, tamed and named mobs and protected regions are not targets by default.
- Turret targeting menu: each turret has its own menu, for its owner only. It holds the info, load and eject buttons, the friendly-fire
  switch, four category toggles (monsters, phantoms, slimes and ghasts) and 18 wool toggles, one per entity type. An entity toggle
  beats its category, which beats the config. Owners only; the `corefactory.turret` permission applies.
- Friends: `/cf friends add`, `remove` and `list` keep each player's friend list in `friends.yml`, next to `turrets.yml`. A friend
  is never a turret target. WorldGuard region members and owners, and GriefPrevention players trusted with Access or Manage in the
  claim where a turret stands, count as friends too. Both claim checks fail closed.
- Turret friendly fire: a per-turret switch, off by default, in the menu and as `/cf turret friendlyfire <on|off>` (the turret
  you look at within 6 blocks, or your nearest turret within 8 blocks). On, the turret fires at strangers, the players who are
  neither its owner nor a friend. Off, it fires at no player.
- Splitter filter drag and shift-click: a drag of an item from your inventory over a list adds its type to that list, once, and a
  shift-click adds it to the first list with room (left for a left-click, right for a right-click).
- Machine input drag and shift-click: a machine's input and fuel slots take a drag or a shift-click of an item its processes use,
  the separator included. Output slots refuse both.

- Turret ammunition: regular rounds are the baseline; armour-piercing rounds ignore a share of armour; incendiary rounds set the
  target on fire; depleted-uranium rounds have a tighter spread and ignore some armour.
- Catalogue: two categories, Ammunition and Defences, between Splitters and Ores (17 categories in all). The new process machines
  are in Lab machines.
- Ore blocks: saltpeter, sulfur, zinc and coal ore, with their own textures and host blocks (lime, orange and cyan glazed
  terracotta, and coal ore).
- 3D models and animated textures for the tall generators, drills IV and V, the refinery, the solar panel, the particle
  accelerator, the antimatter condenser and the eight process machines.
- PIN lock for nuclear TNT. The owner can sneak-right-click a placed nuclear TNT to set a 4-digit PIN on a keypad. A locked
  TNT does not detonate from any trigger and cannot be broken by a player until it is unlocked with the PIN; a right-click
  opens the unlock keypad. Locks are optional, unlocked by default, and saved with the TNT in `placed-tnt.yml` as a salted
  hash. Three wrong PINs lock the keypad for 30 seconds (`locks` in `config.yml`). Permissions `corefactory.lock` and
  `corefactory.lock.admin`. See `docs/MECHANICS.md#pin-lock`.
- The resource pack is bundled inside the plugin jar, under `resource-pack/`. When the plugin loads, it copies the pack to
  `plugins/CoreFactory/pack/pack.zip` if it is newer than the copy there, and it loads before ResourcePackManager (`loadbefore`),
  which reads that file. The copy is made in `onLoad`, before any plugin is enabled.
- The bundled pack carries its version and build (`corefactory-pack.properties`). On start the plugin replaces the installed
  pack only when the bundled one is newer, and it never downgrades a newer pack. `/cf pack` shows the state, and
  `/cf pack update` applies the rule now.
- Achievements: 364 of them with the shipped data (3 per TNT type, 2 per component, 19 counters, 30 science feats and 7
  mastery groups), built from the TNT types and components. Each TNT is forged, lit and detonated; each component
  is forged and used; there are counters, chain and nuclear feats (including a village nuke and surviving a blast), and
  seven mastery groups. Progress is per player, saved in `achievements.yml`, and shown in `/cf achievements`.
  See `docs/ACHIEVEMENTS.md`.
- `/cf book`: the tutorial book, a written book with a short guide.
- `/cf recipe <id>`: a recipe in a crafting grid. Right-clicking a catalogue entry opens the same view.
- Procedural texture generator (`io.corefactory.resourcepack.art`). Every TNT and component has its own 32 × 32 texture,
  painted by code from a palette, a pictogram, a band and a label. `ArtGenerator` writes the PNGs, a YAML descriptor per
  item and a contact sheet. Textures are deterministic and unique, and a test checks both.
- `docs/TEXTURES.md`: the specification, the tools and the processes for changing, adding and checking textures.
- `ClientEffects.setClientWeather`: rain and thunder shown to one player.
- Nuclear fallout: a dose builds for a player standing in it, with nausea from 1 Sv and health loss from 2 Sv. Fallout
  lasts for hours (zone half-life 90 minutes). See `docs/MECHANICS.md`.
- Radioactive Suit: four craftable armour pieces made from Hazmat Fabric. Worn in full, they shield the fallout entirely.
- Geiger Counter: a held tool that shows the local dose rate, its radiation type and the dose taken on the action bar.
- Science chain (`docs/SCIENCE.md`, `processes.yml`, `materials.yml`): seven ores (uraninite, pitchblende, monazite,
  thorite, galena, cinnabar, fluorite) that are roasted, separated, converted to UF6, enriched to U-235, and transmuted
  to U-233 and plutonium. Eleven machines, each with its own inventory screen and placed 3D model: crusher, roasting kiln,
  centrifugal separator, purification still, fluorination converter, enrichment cascade, neutron transmuter,
  spectrometer, particle accelerator, antimatter condenser, research console.
- Ore deposits: deposits are planned from the world seed, each a circular patch of one ore with a remaining amount, in cells
  of 160 blocks (by default). Each chunk is laid once, when it first loads. The `deposits` section of `config.yml` holds the
  planning constants, and `/cf geology deposits [radius]` lists the deposits near you (`corefactory.geology`).
  See `docs/MECHANICS.md#deposits`.
- Research (`research.yml`): a linear tree of fifteen nodes. Crafting, placing and lighting a gated item needs its node
  finished at a Research Console. Progress is saved permanently: in SQLite (`research.db`, one server) or in PostgreSQL
  (shared by several servers, set in `config.yml`). Storage uses HikariCP.
- Exotic Matter Series: an exotic neutron bomb (a 5 kt blast whose radiation dose reaches far beyond it) and an antimatter
  bomb (about 180 Mt). The 4-dimensional bomb is reworked to about a gigaton, the strongest weapon in the game.
- Radiation suit and Geiger counter art, and machine models, come from the art pipeline (see `docs/TEXTURES.md`).
- Lab tools keep their items in a barrel under them. Hoppers feed the inputs and the fuel and take only the outputs. Inputs
  and fuel never leave by hopper, and a lab tool's menu is a view of the barrel.
- Barrel concealment: the barrel under each lab tool and drill is hidden from nearby players, who see the custom model
  instead. Each machine has an invisible interaction hitbox: right-click opens it and left-click breaks it. Config:
  `concealment.enabled` (true) and `concealment.range` (48).
- Progress holograms over running lab tools and drills, sent as packets. A lab tool's hologram has three lines (the process, a bar, and the percentage with the time left or `idle`) and goes when the tool is idle. A drill's has five lines (its name and level, its power, its rate or status, the deposit it works on, and its storage and fuel) and shows while the drill has power and room. Each line is its own invisible armour stand. Config: `holograms.enabled` and `holograms.view-distance`.
- Ore Density Scanner rework: a held item that crafts and unlocks after Laboratory Basics (`materials.yml`). Right-click it in air or on a block to scan the deposits within `deposits.scanner-radius` (96 blocks), with `deposits.scan-cooldown-ticks` (60) between two scans. The action bar names the nearest deposit with its direction, distance, richness and what is left (`Nearest Galena Ore: 42 m NE, Rich, 3.2M left`), and the chat lists up to five. Sneak and right-click cycles the ore filter (all ores, each ore, then all). Sneak and left-click surveys the radius: the chat gives the exact centre and radius of up to ten deposits, and it never breaks a block. A scan or survey outlines the nearest eight deposits in their ore's colour for `deposits.outline-seconds` (20). Richness is Poor under 1,000,000 left, Rich from 1,000,000 and Exceptional from 10,000,000 (`RichnessTier`). The scanner keys are read on their own. See `docs/PLAYER_GUIDE.md#the-ore-density-scanner`.
- Deposit map and waypoint: `/cf deposits` (`corefactory.geology`) opens the nearest deposits of the scanner's filter, 18 to a page and up to 54, each with its direction, distance, richness and share left. A click on a deposit sets the player's waypoint: a gold beam points toward it, the action bar shows its direction and distance, it lasts two minutes, and it ends with an arrival message inside the deposit. The map has a filter button and a clear-waypoint button. The map needs `corefactory.geology`, which is for operators by default. `/cf geology deposits` lists the same chat line for every deposit in its radius, with no limit.
- Hand-mining deposits: breaking a deposit's patch block by hand gives one item of its ore (the component: raw minerals, charcoal for coal, petroleum for crude petroleum) and takes one unit from that deposit. Silk touch gives the same item. An empty deposit gives nothing, and the block still breaks. A block you cannot break gives nothing, so claims and protection apply. Hand-mined patch blocks count toward the mining achievements when they give their item (`GeologyListener`, `PatchBlocks`). See `docs/MECHANICS.md#deposits`.
- Patch exclusions: a host block that a player places on a patch site, that a piston moves onto one, or that a dispenser puts on one is excluded from being a patch block, so it gives no ore even on a mined-out site. The exclusions are kept per chunk in its persistent data (`PatchExclusions`). Blasts give no ore either. Host blocks already in the stone when a chunk is laid, such as natural coal ore, are excluded too.
- Petroleum drills (five levels) pump petroleum from crude deposits, on the strongest of a solar panel, redstone or charcoal. Ore drills (five levels)
  mine ore deposits and run on petroleum. Each level has its own area, depth and cycle, and a drill stands on hard base
  stone. A drill holds its items in its barrel and takes fuel from a hopper.
- Solar panel: a daylight detector placed as a block. Its power, from its redstone level, runs a petroleum drill next to it.
- Drill power and status: a petroleum drill runs at the power of its strongest source, a solar panel (its power over 15), redstone (its power over 15) or charcoal (0.35). The source is named on the hologram and in the info item. An ore drill runs at full power on petroleum. The status lines are No power (No petroleum for an ore drill), Output full, No deposit in range, Deposit exhausted: no items left in range and Running. Rates are shown per second and per minute.
- Crude petroleum deposit, an ore laid as deposits. A petroleum drill draws the deposit down, one petroleum per unit taken,
  so the deposit is used up rather than kept.
- Ore blocks: each special ore is a placeable block item. A placed ore block gives its raw mineral when mined; silk touch gives
  the block item back. A patch block dug by hand gives one raw item from its deposit (see Hand-mining deposits above). `/cf give` accepts ore ids.
- Catalogue (`/cf gui`): a paged browser of every TNT, component, machine, ore and research pack, in fifteen categories, each its own tab. Browsing
  needs `corefactory.guide`. Right-click for a recipe, and in the recipe view click an ingredient with a recipe of its own
  to open it. BACK returns.
- `/cf research max [player]`, `/cf research grant <node> [player]` and `/cf research nodes`, for administrators
  (`corefactory.research.admin`, operators by default).
- Particle effects: 22 named sprite effects for blasts, the mushroom cloud and ground surge, fallout, neutron bursts,
  antimatter flashes, the tesseract fold, machine work, lab operations, research completion and ore breaks. Config:
  `effects.enabled` and `effects.max-sprites` (600).
- Startup data merge: an entry missing from a data file is added from the shipped defaults on start. Each rewritten file
  keeps a `.bak` copy. Existing entries are never overwritten, and an entry that differs from the default is logged.
- Storage: research in SQLite (one server) or PostgreSQL (shared), set in the `storage` section. The JDBC driver is not in
  the jar. It is downloaded from Maven Central on first use and checked against its published SHA-1. HikariCP is bundled.
  The deposits' remaining amounts are stored with the research data, in the same database.
- Electricity (`docs/MECHANICS.md`, `docs/PLAYER_GUIDE.md`): three pylon sizes, each with a square range and a maximum current;
  the energy wire, which joins two pylons, or a generator to a pylon, over up to 24 blocks; three accumulator levels that store power and give
  it back when a line runs short; six generators (wind, solar, charcoal, petroleum, diesel and nuclear), each with a menu and a
  hologram; the crude oil refinery, which distils three petroleum into two diesel; and `/cf pylons show [seconds]`, which draws
  the pylon ranges near the player (`corefactory.pylons`). The network is saved in `energy.yml`.
- Logistics (`docs/MECHANICS.md`, `docs/PLAYER_GUIDE.md`): transport belts, pickers and splitters, each in three levels. A picker
  moves one item from its input to its output. A splitter sends each item straight on, or left or right by its filter, set in a
  panel with a left list and a right list of up to nine types each. Belts, pickers and splitters draw power from the grid. The
  state is saved in `logistics.yml`.

- Grid charts: `/cf grid create <name>` makes a hologram of the grid you stand in, one block above your head. It shows the
  grid's satisfaction, production or storage. `remove`, `list`, `mode <name> <satisfaction|production|storage>` and `info`
  manage them. A chart refreshes every 10 ticks while a player is within 48 blocks. There is one chart per grid and five on
  the server (`charts.charts-per-grid`, `charts.charts-total`). Creating, removing, changing the mode and the right-click need
  `corefactory.grid` (operators by default); `list` and `info` need `corefactory.pylons`. See `docs/MECHANICS.md#grid-charts`.
- Logistics ports: a lab tool's barrel, a drill's output slots and a generator's fuel are ports for belts and pickers
  (`LabPort`, `DrillPort`, `FuelPort`). A drill's port gives its output slots, first slot first, and takes nothing; its fuel
  still comes from a hopper or by hand.

- Roasting kiln: a firebox indicator on the kiln's screen, between the feed slots and the fuel slot. It shows blaze powder while the kiln burns fuel, and gunpowder while it does not.
- Device models: lab machines, drills, pylons, accumulators, generators, the refinery, pickers, splitters and belts are placed as 3D models. One face of each pylon, accumulator, generator, the refinery and the solar panel is animated, and the belt tops scroll.
- Catalogue categories: the catalogue has fifteen categories, each a tab of its own (nuclear warheads, exotic matter series, other TNT, crafting parts, radiation gear, science materials, research packs, lab machines, drills, electricity, generators, belts, pickers, splitters and ores). When the tabs do not fit, arrows in the first and last slots of the tab row scroll them, seven at a time.
- External ignition of placed custom TNT: fire, lava, lightning, flint and steel, fire charges, flaming arrows, fire spread, redstone power and vanilla explosions light it (`IgnitionListener`, `IgnitionRules`). An ignition that another plugin cancelled stays cancelled.
- Colour formatting for help and tab completion: help headings are gold and bold, commands aqua, arguments yellow, descriptions gray and separators dark gray, with green for confirmations and red for errors (`Style`). Tab-completion suggestions stay plain text, and only the ones the sender may use are offered (`Completion`).
- Laser turret: an electric turret that fires an instant beam at the mobs a machine gun turret fires at, from 40 blocks, for 12 damage a shot that ignores armour. It holds no rounds, so belts, pickers and hoppers cannot feed it, and its menu has no load or eject buttons. It asks for 60 kW, and its settings are the `laser-turret` section of `config.yml`.
- Laser turret parts and recipes: the optical lens (metal lathe, from an amethyst shard and high-purity graphite, two per pass), the laser emitter (component assembly, from two circuit boards, glowstone dust and a brass ingot) and the laser turret (heavy weapons assembly, from a steel frame, a targeting unit, a turret mechanism, four optical lenses and two laser emitters, 1400 ticks at 250 kW). The lens and the emitter are researched at Materials Purification, and the turret at Tesseract Geometry.
- Laser light: a laser shot makes its model glow at full brightness, and a white light block stands in the air in front of its barrel, for 4 ticks. The beam is red dust with an END_ROD burst on a hit (`LaserLight`, `LaserEffects`).

- Turret upgrade levels: each turret has five levels, and a kit moves a turret you own up one level when you right-click it while holding the kit. Each level sets the damage boost, fire interval, range, belt capacity (machine gun), cannons and power, from the `levels` blocks of `config.yml`. Levels 3 to 5 are two blocks tall. The move to level 3 needs the block above free; a turret already tall owns the block above it.
- Upgrade kits II to V (`turret_upgrade_2` to `turret_upgrade_5`): kits II and III are made in the component assembly and researched at Materials Purification; kits IV and V are made in the heavy weapons assembly and researched at Tesseract Geometry.
- Dual cannons: a machine gun at levels 4 and 5 has two cannons, each with its own target, and both fire in the same tick. The second cannon prefers a mob within `turrets.dual-spacing-blocks` (6 blocks) of the first, and takes the nearest free mob in range when none is that close.
- Turret target coordination: the turrets of one world share out the mobs. Each cannon takes the nearest mob that no other turret has claimed and keeps it while it stays in reach, and with more turrets than mobs the extra turrets share them. The pass runs every `turrets.search-interval-ticks` and on the tick after a target dies (`TargetAssignment`).
- Spherical range: a turret's range is a sphere around its barrel, measured to the nearest point of a mob's box, so a large mob at the edge of the range is hit.
- Turret targeting commands: `/cf turret info`, `target <mob|category> <on|off>`, `player <name> <target|spare|clear>`, `team <spare|target|ignore>` and `friendlyfire <on|off>`, for the turret you look at within 6 blocks or your nearest turret within 8 blocks. Owner only, under `corefactory.turret`, with tab completion.
- Team modes and player stances: the owner's scoreboard team is spared as friends, targeted like strangers, or ignored, by the menu's team switch or `/cf turret team`. A single player can be set to target (even with friendly fire off) or spare, with `/cf turret player`. The owner is never a target.
- Turret menu and hologram: the info item shows the level and the stats of that level, the menu has a team switch, and the hologram shows the level as `L1` to `L5`.
- Level 5 laser: its beam, its impact and its lens are blue. Levels 1 to 4 are red.
- Turret models for every level: `machine_gun_turret_2` to `_5` and `laser_turret_2` to `_5`, with `_top` halves for the tall levels 3 to 5, and an animated front on each level.
- Tall turrets: a turret at level 3 to 5 is two blocks high, like the tall devices. Breaking either block breaks the whole turret.
  The protection plugins are asked about both blocks, so a claim or region that refuses the upper block refuses the placement, an
  upgrade into it, and the break.
- Tall drills: a level IV or V drill asks the protection plugins about the block above when it is placed. A refusal stops the
  placement with "This spot is protected."
- Containers: a belt, a picker or a splitter can deliver into a chest, a trapped chest, a barrel, a shulker box or a copper chest
  (every copper chest type). The container fills until it is full, and then the belt waits.
- Picker input from storage: a picker can take from a chest, a trapped chest, a barrel, a shulker box, a copper chest or either half
  of a double chest on its input side, the whole double inventory being used. It takes one item at a time, at its rate, from the
  first plain stack. A named or enchanted item is skipped.
- Lab tool fuel from belts: a belt or a picker feeds a lab tool's fuel slot as well as its inputs. A kiln's fuel slot takes coal,
  vanilla charcoal and the charcoal that broken coal ore drops, by click, drag, shift-click, hopper, belt or picker.

### Changed

- Ore placement is rebuilt as deposit regions: circular patches of one ore with a remaining amount, planned from the world seed,
  laid into each chunk once on load, and stored in `corefactory_deposits`. Drills mine deposits, one at a time, at their level's
  rate (`drill-rates`: 1.0 to 6.0 items per second at full power). The `deposits` section of `config.yml` holds the planning
  constants. See `docs/MECHANICS.md#deposits` and `docs/MECHANICS.md#drills`.
- Turret scan rate: `turrets.search-interval-ticks` defaults to 20 ticks, one second. A lower value scans more often and costs more
  server time. Allowed: 1 to 1200.
- Turret arc and tilt: the arc defaults to 360 degrees and the tilt limit to 90, the whole sphere. A `config.yml` that sets
  `arc-degrees: 180` keeps it: change it to 360, change `pitch-limit-degrees` to 90, and restart.
- Turret range: a turret fires at every hostile mob in its sphere, in every direction, unless a solid block is in the line of fire.
  Grass, flowers and snow layers do not block a shot.
- Turret sounds: the idle sweep is silent. The rotation sound plays only while a turret turns onto a target it has detected, and the
  fire sound plays on each shot.
- Turret owner control: only the owner, with `corefactory.turret`, can load, eject, upgrade, change the targeting of, or break a
  turret. Anyone else can open its menu and look, but cannot touch it.
- Container claim rule: a belt, a picker or a splitter placed after this update records its placer, and delivers into a container
  only where that placer may use it: in their own claim, where they are trusted with Inventory, Container, Build, Edit or Manage
  (GriefPrevention), or in a WorldGuard region where they are a member or an owner. A device with no owner, such as one placed before
  this update, is refused in any claim or region, and so is a container fed by two different owners.
- Claim plugins fail closed: when an installed WorldGuard or GriefPrevention hook cannot attach, every container is closed to
  devices. With neither plugin installed, every container is open.
- Chunk unload: when a chunk unloads, a kiln or lab menu open in it is closed, and its pending edits are written to the machine
  first, so the machine keeps what was put in.
- Turret stats moved into the level table: `range`, `firing-interval-ticks`, `ammo-capacity` and `power-demand-w` are no longer read from `turrets`, and `range`, `damage`, `firing-interval-ticks` and `power-demand-w` are no longer read from `laser-turret`. The `levels` blocks set them, and level 1 keeps the old values.
- Rounds deal the level's damage: every round hits for its turret's damage (6 for a machine gun at level 1), and the round sets only its armour bypass, fire and spread. Armour-piercing used to deal 7, incendiary 5 and depleted uranium 9. The `damage` key under `turrets.rounds` is read but not used.
- Turret and laser sounds are quieter: the pack volume is about a quarter of its earlier value, and the server plays them at volume 0.35.
- Machine gun turret model: a placed turret showed a missing-texture cube; it now has a 3D model (`machine/machine_gun_turret`) with an animated amber front, and it turns toward its target.
- Sounds: the pack has 40 sounds. Nine are CC0 recordings: turret fire and reload, the laser's fire and charge, the diesel engine, the generator hum, the press stamp, the purifier and the induction hiss. Sources: OpenGameArt "gunshots" (kurt, LarkPay), "Gun reload sounds" (SpringySpringo, Joaken), "63 digital sound effects" (Kenney Vleugels, kenney.nl), "Engine sounds" (pauliuw), "Generator loop" (YCbCr), "Blacksmith's hammer" (VishwaJai), "Bubble sound effects" (BMacZero) and "Electronic device loop" (qubodup). The other 31 are procedural, including the rewritten turret rotation. The credits are in `docs/CREDITS.md`.
- Data version 4: `processes.yml` is version 4 (version 5 below supersedes it). A server with an older file gets the shipped one on start and keeps the old file as `processes.yml.bak`. Existing `research.yml` nodes are not changed, so the laser parts are ungated on such a server until the file is updated.
- Wire rendering redesigned: a wire is a thin catenary sag in its grid's colour (no longer grey dust every 10 ticks), with a
  travelling pulse from the generator end to the pylon end whose count and speed follow the grid's satisfaction, and none when
  idle. Wires are drawn every 5 ticks within `energy.wire-view-blocks` (24) of a player, capped per player by
  `energy.wire-particle-cap` (320; 0 turns wires off).
- Data version 5: `processes.yml` is version 5 (`DataVersion.CURRENT`), with the four turret upgrade kits and their processes. A server with an older file gets the shipped one on start, and keeps the old file as `processes.yml.bak`.
- `processes.yml` is data version 5 (see the data version item above). A server whose copy is older or has no version line gets the shipped file, and its old file
  is kept as `processes.yml.bak`. Edits made to the old file are in that backup.
- Note for server admins: the new items and machines are gated across the existing research nodes in `research.yml`. A server
  that already has a `research.yml` keeps its old node unlocks, because the startup merge does not overwrite a node. Remove the
  node from that file to take the new unlocks, or grant them with `/cf research`.
- Generators and drills of level IV and V are two blocks tall. A single-block generator or drill from before this change that now
  stands tall may have a block placed above it, and its display may overlap that block. There is no automatic migration.
- The texture blocks are gone from `tnts.yml` and `items.yml`. The look of every item is defined in the Java painters,
  and the pack builder reads the generated descriptors instead. The old 16 × 16 renderer is removed.
- Nuclear craters are now shaped like the real thing. Tiers 1 to 5 (surface bursts) cut a shallow saucer with a
  compacted floor and fused glass at the centre. Tiers 6 to 10 (thermonuclear) cut a deep hemisphere with a sinking rim.
  Both clear everything above the ground out to `flatten-radius`. The old `crater-fraction` setting is replaced by
  `shape`, `crater-radius`, `crater-depth`, `sink-depth` and `flatten-radius`.
- Nuclear radii are several times larger (Davy Crockett 6 to 14 blocks, Tsar Bomba 150 to 220). Only the nuclear family
  changed; other TNT types keep their radii.
- Nuclear damage has three zones: an instant-death fireball (0.3 R), a blast area with falling damage and long fire
  (out to R, reduced by blast protection and the suit), and a fallout out to 3 R. The countdown and detonation titles name
  the zone. `fire-far-seconds` and `fire-near-seconds` are replaced by `blast-fire-seconds`, and each tier has a
  `yield-megatons`.
- The beacon beams are sent only for yields of 10 Mt or more. Smaller tiers show the rain and clock change only.
- Enriched uranium is no longer crafted from netherite scrap: it is an output of the enrichment cascade. The plutonium pit
  is pressed from plutonium oxide, and the tritium booster uses deuterium.
- The Tsar Bomba (50 Mt) is no longer the strongest weapon: the antimatter bomb and the 4-dimensional bomb are larger.
- The shade plugin is pinned to 3.6.1. Version 3.6.0 cannot read Java 25 class files and fails to package the plugin.
- Lab tools and ore drills that an earlier build kept on another block (a dispenser, a furnace, a dropper and the like) move
  to a barrel once their chunk loads. The old block's contents drop at its position. A stack with no empty barrel slot stays in
  `machines.yml` and is logged, so nothing is overwritten.
- The machine textures moved to `textures/block/machine/<id>/`, so the block atlas holds them. Block textures go under
  `textures/block`, and item textures under `textures/item`. See `docs/RESOURCE_PACK.md`.
- The pack adds ten drill models, with level stripes, and the solar panel, scanner, petroleum and crude petroleum deposit art.
- The solar panel is a generator now. A new one is placed on air and makes up to 320 W at noon and nothing at night; it
  feeds the grid through wires. Solar panels placed before this change are still daylight detectors, and they still power the
  petroleum drill beside them.
- Lab tools and drills draw power from the grid. A lab tool with no pylon covering it stops, and otherwise runs at the share
  of power its network gives it (`energy.consumers.lab-demand`; 0 restores the old behaviour). A drill runs on the stronger of
  its own power and its network (`energy.consumers.drill-demand`). Their holograms gain a `Grid` line.
- The electrical model is in real units. Power is in watts (W, kW, MW), energy in watt-hours (Wh, kWh), voltage in volts
  (230 V nominal) and current in amperes. A pylon I carries 100 A, which is 23 kW; a pylon II 400 A, 92 kW; a pylon III
  1600 A, 368 kW. Accumulators hold 5, 20 and 80 kWh and charge or discharge at 5, 20 and 60 kW. Wind makes up to 3 kW, solar
  up to 320 W, charcoal 25 kW, petroleum 80 kW, diesel 150 kW and nuclear 2 MW. Lab tools ask for 4 kW
  (`energy.consumers.lab-demand`, 4000 W), drills for 15 to 90 kW by level (`energy.consumers.drill-demand`, 15000 W), belts for
  0.2 to 0.8 kW, pickers for 0.1 to 0.4 kW and splitters for 0.5 to 1.5 kW.
- `energy.yml` is version 2: it holds the next grid id, each pylon's grid, the charts, and energy in Wh with times in seconds.
  A file from before this version resets the stored accumulator energy, the burn time of the generators and their produced
  totals to zero, and the console says so once. Positions, levels, fuel, wires and pylons are kept.
- Grids. A grid is a group of pylons joined by wires, with the generators wired to them. Pylons can be wired to each other,
  and to a generator; two generators, and any consumer or accumulator, are refused. A grid keeps a persistent id and a colour from a palette of
  16. Its range is the union of its pylons' squares, and grids that overlap add up in the overlap. A wire is at most 24 blocks.
- Outlines. `/cf pylons show [seconds]` draws the outline of each grid in the grid's colour, for the grids with a pylon within
  96 blocks of you. The default is 15 seconds and the most is 120. It replaces the blue squares within 64 blocks.
- Holograms. A pylon's hologram has two lines (its grid, voltage and current; the power and the capacity). An accumulator's
  has two lines. A generator's has one. The `Grid` line of a lab tool and a drill gives the grid's voltage, the power the
  device asks for and its share, and it is always shown.
- Belt arrows: the arrowheads on each belt top point toward the bottom edge of the texture, and they scroll that way. The in-game check is still pending; see `docs/COMPATIBILITY.md`.
- Explosions destroy devices: a blast removes every lab tool, drill, pylon, accumulator, generator, belt, picker and splitter whose block centre lies within its radius (3D distance), unless protection spares the block. Vanilla explosions use their own radii: creeper 3 (6 when powered), end crystal 6, fireball and wither skull 1, any other entity 4, and a bed or respawn anchor 5. Lab tools and drills used to be left standing.
- `processes.yml` carries a version line (`# data-version: 2`). On start, a copy with an older or missing version is kept as `processes.yml.bak` and replaced by the shipped file; a newer copy is left alone with a warning. Its machines, processes and drills are not merged entry by entry any more.
- The wire refusal names the wires that are allowed: a generator to a pylon, or two pylons. A wire to a consumer or an accumulator, and a wire between two generators, are refused.
- Dual spacing is a preference, not a limit: when no unclaimed mob lies within `turrets.dual-spacing-blocks` of the first target, the second cannon takes the nearest unclaimed mob in range, and it no longer waits.
- Red flash: a mob flashes red when it takes any hit, and a turret's hit is one. A mob that burns from an incendiary round flashes red from the fire, which is not a hit.
- Picker claim rule for taking: a picker takes from a container on its input side only where its owner may use it, by the rule for deliveries: the owner's own claim, a GriefPrevention trust level of Inventory, Container, Build, Edit or Manage, or a WorldGuard region membership. A picker with no stored owner takes nothing from a claimed container, and a double chest counts as claimed if either half is. Splitters still take from the belt behind them only.
- Turret power: a turret, machine gun or laser, asks for its level's power only while a cannon holds a target. Otherwise it asks for 1 W of standby (`TurretService.consumers`).

### Removed

- Removed systems: the vein-based ore placement (and the `veins-per-chunk` and `vein-size` keys, which are no longer read), the
  chunk budgets and their retrofit (`/cf geology retrofit`, `/cf geology status`, and `geology.chunks-per-tick`), the mining
  ledger, and the old ore survey. The scanner's earlier map and chat summary are replaced by the scanner rework under Added.
- Schema migration 4 drops the `corefactory_mining_ledger` and `corefactory_chunk_budget` tables when the database opens (`SchemaMigrations`). No code reads or writes them.

### Fixed

- Turrets target magma cubes when their magma cube toggle or the slimes category is on. A magma cube is not a slime in the server API (it is a cube mob of its own), so the turret had classed it as "other" and never fired at it.

- Turret upgrades from level 3 to 4 and from 4 to 5 now work. The block above a tall turret belongs to the turret, so the upgrade
  no longer counts it as blocked. Level III (kit III) still needs the block above it free.
- Belt corners: the 90-degree arc on a corner's top texture now starts at the input side and ends at the output side, which is the front, the bottom edge of the top texture. The arrows follow the arc. The check in game is still pending (see `docs/COMPATIBILITY.md`).
- The right-hand belt corner rendered black and inside out. Its model used a mirrored display, which flips the faces. It is now
  drawn with a mirrored texture (`corner_right`), and the display is not mirrored.
- Pickers and splitters are placed on a side face of a machine: north, south, east or west, never the top or the bottom. The
  face you aim at is the face that touches the machine. The input faces the machine and the output faces away, so the arrows
  point outward, and no rotation is needed. A left-click with a belt, picker or splitter in hand no longer breaks it.
- Drill depths are 48, 96, 144, 200 and 280 layers for levels I to V (they were 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64). The depth is shown on the
  info item, but it no longer limits which deposits a drill finds; only the square does. The status of a drill with no deposit in
  its square reads `No deposit in range`, for both kinds, and `Deposit exhausted: no items left in range` when every deposit in
  range is empty.
- A picker beside a drill takes the drill's products from its output slots, first slot first, through `DrillPort`.
- The achievements tab background was a missing texture (a vanilla path that does not resolve). The pack now ships its own
  tile at `textures/gui/advancements/background.png`, and the tab points at it.
- The datapack format is also read from the 26.1 form `[101, 1]`, which was not understood before and left the datapack
  unwritten on 26.1 servers.
- The achievements datapack used the old plural folder `advancements/`. Since 1.21 the server reads the singular
  `advancement/`, so the datapack loaded but registered no advancements. It is now written to `advancement/`.
- Crafting recipes of the plugin were dropped by data reloads (enabling or disabling a datapack), which showed "Tried to load
  unrecognized recipe". The plugin now checks the recipes every two seconds and registers any that are missing.
- Achievements now appear in the Advancements screen under a CoreFactory tab, with the TNT and component models as icons.
  The server announces each unlock to everyone through its own toast and chat, and counters fill up in the tab. The
  achievements are written to the datapack `CoreFactory-achievements`. If the tab does not show after the first start, run
  `datapack enable "file/CoreFactory-achievements"` in the console.
- Fixed the datapack being rejected by the server ("missing mandatory fields min_format and max_format"). The pack now
  declares the same `min_format`/`max_format` range as the server's built-in data packs, and it is written before the worlds
  load.
- Placed custom TNT showed the vanilla texture. The item model had lost its full-size "fixed" display transform when
  the pack builder was rewritten, so the item display drew at half size inside the vanilla block. The transform is set
  again, and a test checks it.
- The textures were missing in game. The resource pack declared `pack_format` 75 (Minecraft 1.21.11) for every server,
  so a 26.x client did not apply it. The pack format is now set per Maven profile: 84 for 26.1, 88 for 26.2, 97 for
  26.3 and 75 for 1.21.11. Packs built before this change must be rebuilt and re-hosted.
- Beacon beams for nuclear blasts were not drawn. A client draws a beam only from a beacon with a pyramid, and the
  array had none. Each beacon now sits on a client-side 3 × 3 iron platform. The beam scan also stopped at the beacon
  itself, so each column gets a client-side light block at the top of the world, and the beam runs to the sky. The
  platform hangs one block above the ground, because the blast's own ground changes would overwrite it.
- The client clock did not hold at noon: the server re-sent the real time every second and undid a plain packet. The
  outgoing time packets are now rewritten for that player while the hold lasts, and the clock slides back to the real
  time over 20 seconds.
- The rain now stops for players within 3 times the radius during the blast, and comes back afterwards.
- The ore drill crash on a dropper host. A drill needs ten slots (its fuel slot and nine outputs), and a dropper has nine. Ore drills now stand on a barrel, which has 27 slots.
- Petroleum drill power. Redstone and solar power were ignored, and charcoal's 35 percent fallback was shown as the drill's power. The power is now the strongest source, and its name is shown.
- Hologram length. The text of a hologram is kept in short lines, one armour stand for each line.
- Corner belts take their turn from the corner model's input port (`Orientation.cornerYaw`), so a corner faces the side its items
  come from. The in-game check is pending; see `docs/COMPATIBILITY.md`.
- Turret placement no longer refuses a valid spot with "This spot is protected": a placement-guard bug, now fixed.
- Turret shots are no longer blocked by Minecraft's short invulnerability after a hit (about half a second). Each turret hit clears that window before it deals damage, so every shot lands, and only the turret's own fire interval separates its shots. The hit still uses the normal damage call, so armour, protection and damage events apply.
- A failure while one world's logistics runs is logged, and the rest of the logistics keeps running. The failed world runs again on the next tick.
- Picker and splitter at partial power: the time a tick overshoots an item's interval now carries into the next one, so a picker or splitter at satisfaction s moves s times its rate, with no item lost to tick rounding.
- Laser and machine gun targets: a laser pass and a machine gun pass each avoid a mob the other kind holds. Such a mob is taken only when no other mob is free for the cannon, so a laser does not take a machine gun's mob while another is free.
- Drill deposit hand-over: when a drill moves to another deposit, it keeps the old one until the items it made for it are in the outputs, so no item is dropped for want of room.
- Turret damage count: a turret's damage dealt (and the kill blow in it) counts only the health the mob lost, read after the blow. A cancelled damage event or a resistance can leave a mob standing, and then only the health it did lose is counted.
- Drill search reach: the search covers the largest radius a deposit may have plus the drill's square, so the largest deposits are found. A lowered `max-radius` no longer shortens it.
- Drill squares: a square is exactly as wide as its area, for odd areas too. An odd area is centred, and an even area puts the extra block on the low side.
- Turret feeding: a round that a belt or a picker puts into a turret is saved at once, as every other turret change is.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-10-18

### Added

- 40 TNT types across ten families, each with its own mechanic, radius, fuse and settings: structural and physics
  (7), terrain morphing (5), entity and status (4), timing and logic (4), visual and audio (4), utility and economy
  (4), military (2), and a ten-tier nuclear family.
- 18 crafting components, with recipes and textures. Nuclear and the strongest TNT need them.
- Shapeless and shaped recipes for every TNT and component, defined in `tnts.yml` and `items.yml`.
- Placed-TNT registry with persistence; per-block custom display; fuse links; directions; teleport targets.
- Fuse Linker tool for linking placed TNT.
- The `/cf` command: give, list, catalogue GUI, info, aim, target, unlink, count, reload.
- Staged block pipeline with WorldEdit or FastAsyncWorldEdit, streaming generators for large craters, and budgets
  in `config.yml`.
- PacketEvents-based client-only effects: per-player daytime, and a 5x5 array of client-side beacons for the nuclear beams.
- Optional WorldGuard (TNT flag) and GriefPrevention (claims) integration, with per-tick caching and an on/off switch per plugin.
- A resource pack generator that draws a texture per TNT and component.
- Modules: Api, Core, Spigot, ResourcePack. Maven profiles for Spigot-API 26.1, 26.2, 26.3 and 1.21.11.
- Documentation under `docs/`.

### Fixed before release

- Cancelled explosions no longer ignite placed TNT; chains respect other plugins' decisions.
- Placed TNT displays are centred on their block.
- A refused detonation returns its TNT item; the chain-depth limit is enforced.
- Placed TNT and fuse links are kept for worlds that load later, and are cleaned up on break and overwrite.
- `/cf target` takes coordinates; proximity arming persists across restarts.
- A failing block job is abandoned without stalling the queue.
- Flint and steel (and fire charges) on a placed custom TNT now light it through CoreFactory. Before, vanilla lit the block
  and its plain explosion replaced the custom mechanic, which is why most types appeared to do nothing.
- Custom TNT that vanilla primes anyway (arrows, fire, lava) runs its own mechanic instead of a vanilla blast.
- Vitrify no longer throws when it plays its particle effect (FLASH needs a colour).
- `debug: true` logs ignitions, explosions, detonations and blast sizes.

### Verified

- Compiles against all four targets. Unit tests (16) pass.
- Enables on Paper 26.1.2, 26.2, 26.3 and 1.21.11 with WorldEdit 7.4.6 and PacketEvents 2.14.0.
- Gameplay has not been exercised in a client yet; see `docs/COMPATIBILITY.md`.
